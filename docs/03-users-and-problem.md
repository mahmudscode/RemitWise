# 03 — Users and Problem

## Primary persona: the receiving family ("Rahima")
- Household of 4–6 in a district town/village; spouse of a migrant worker.
- Receives transfer via mobile wallet/agent roughly monthly, but timing and amount vary.
- Manages rent/food/school/medicine, informal credit from shops, occasional festival costs.
- Low-to-moderate financial literacy; prefers Bangla, simple screens, possibly voice.
- Pain: "Money comes, I withdraw it all, and by the third week I am borrowing."

## Secondary persona: the sender abroad ("Karim") 
- Works in Gulf/Malaysia/etc., sends money when he can.
- Wants to know goals (house, school fees, business) are progressing, but must not become a surveillance tool.
- Pain: no clear picture of how money is used; guilt and mistrust risk.

## Stakeholder: upay (business view)
- Remittance currently cashed out immediately → low wallet retention.
- Value of RemitWise: money stays in wallet longer, more digital payments, stronger retention, differentiated offering.

## Pain points
1. Irregular timing and amount, no forecast.
2. Full cash-out on arrival → shortfall before next transfer.
3. No savings or emergency buffer despite substantial total income.
4. Shared goals invisible to sender.
5. Existing tools are fixed-income budgeting or generic chatbots.

## Baseline (what we compare against)
- "Same as last time" naive forecast.
- Family behavior of cashing out immediately (simulated).
- Fixed-percentage budget rule (e.g., 50/30/20).

## Day-in-the-life (before → after)
- **Before:** transfer arrives day 5, cash out 90% on day 6, shortfall day ~22, borrow, repay from next transfer.
- **After:** transfer arrives, app splits into needs-until-next-transfer / buffer / goals, keeps it in wallet, scheduled releases, early warning on day 15 if burn rate is high.
